# Example Handover

The prototype includes the following example so a visitor can experience the product without preparing data:

> Marketing normally goes through Michelle. Larger campaigns need Rachel to look at them. If something is urgent, we sometimes skip the usual Finance review. Our PR agency is less formal, so I usually message David directly when a launch is at risk. Legal approval needs to happen before the final launch date is confirmed.

## Why this example is useful

The input intentionally contains multiple forms of operational ambiguity.

### “Marketing normally goes through Michelle.”

Potential ambiguity: **exception + relationship**.

A useful challenge asks when the normal rule does not apply and why Michelle is involved.

### “Larger campaigns need Rachel to look at them.”

Potential ambiguity: **threshold + approval / authority**.

A useful challenge asks what makes a campaign “larger” and whether Rachel is approving the work, budget, timing, or simply being informed.

### “If something is urgent, we sometimes skip the usual Finance review.”

Potential ambiguity: **threshold + exception + authority**.

A useful challenge asks what qualifies as urgent, who can make that call, and whether skipping Finance is an accepted rule or a historical working habit.

### “Our PR agency is less formal, so I usually message David directly when a launch is at risk.”

Potential ambiguity: **preference + relationship + escalation**.

A useful challenge asks what “less formal” changes operationally and what makes a launch sufficiently at risk to contact David directly.

### “Legal approval needs to happen before the final launch date is confirmed.”

Potential ambiguity: **approval + dependency**.

A useful challenge asks what Legal is approving and what happens if that approval is delayed.

## What the example demonstrates

The prototype does not need to know the company, the real stakeholders, or the missing answers in advance. Its role is to recognise where the language is insufficient to guide action and ask the knowledge holder to clarify it.
